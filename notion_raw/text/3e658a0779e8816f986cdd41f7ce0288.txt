Multi-cycle continuation — 2026-09-26
Cycle A — Institutional exchange
Web-verified literature adds an institutional exchange layer. Pandya (1992) frames exchange through institutional context, resource profiles, legitimacy norms and control systems. Ben Slimane et al. (2019) reviews institutional theory in marketing and highlights the need to specify the level at which institutional processes are studied. Prabhu, Tracey & Hassan (2017) show how weak or absent institutions can constrain exchange formation in emerging markets. citeturn300623search4turn300623search0turn300623search8
Derived rule:  institutional conditions are part of the explanatory context for exchange; they are not interchangeable with buyer-level variables or firm-level capabilities.
Cycle B — Public policy interface
The current AMA mission for the Journal of Public Policy & Marketing treats marketing/public policy as a distinct nexus spanning regulation/deregulation, ethics and social responsibility, health/nutrition, ecology, security and privacy. Its research framing is therefore broader than a firm KPI view and requires explicit policy stakeholders and policy contribution when research is intended for this boundary. citeturn353866search1turn353866search5
Derived rule:  policy relevance must be represented as an explicit system/policy layer, not appended as a generic implication to a firm-level result.
Cycle C — Distribution as a system
Stern & Reve (1980) model distribution channels as political economies integrating economic and sociopolitical determinants. Layton (2007) places marketing systems at the center of macromarketing and emphasizes system boundaries, attributes, inputs/outputs, modeling and measurement. citeturn510320search0turn353866search0
Derived rule:  channel analysis should model both economic and sociopolitical governance and must declare the system boundary before selecting measures.
Batch synthesis
The three cycles converge on a layered model:
Actor / Buyer → Organization → Institutional Exchange → Channel / Marketing System → Policy / Society
The layers are connected, but their constructs, mechanisms and evidence types remain distinct. No universal causal hierarchy is inferred.
QC disposition
RSH-12 remains  Needs Follow-up  because the remaining gaps are empirical/application gaps rather than conceptual omissions: applied measurement invariance, digital B2B buying, multi-stakeholder buying groups, and context transfer testing. No Principle promotion was made.
