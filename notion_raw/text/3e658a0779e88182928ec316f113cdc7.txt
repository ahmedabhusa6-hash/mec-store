  Architecture Implication
  Mechanisms are not interchangeable across domains. The same top-level outcome (e.g., loyalty, performance, behavior change) can be reached through different mediators and different boundary conditions. Therefore, the Master Map should store  mechanism chains , not only domain labels.
  Reliability Rules
  - Meta-analytic evidence = strongest current mechanism support in this matrix.
  - Single experiments/empirical studies = local mechanism evidence, not universal law.
  - Review/bibliometric evidence = mechanism candidate or thematic structure unless causal evidence is separately established.
  - Journal scope statements = domain-structure evidence, not causal proof.
  Next Test

  Interpretation:  apparent contradiction resolves as a  context/mediator boundary , not an empirical collapse of the personalization construct. The mechanism must record perceived control, privacy risk, relationship strength and potential embarrassment/identity threat where relevant.
  Exception 2 — Omnichannel / retail
  Existing omnichannel evidence includes both complementary and cannibalizing cross-channel effects. A 2024 grocery field experiment found digital and print promotion support produced different sales pathways, reinforcing that channel effects depend on the channel configuration and target response. citeturn999537search9turn999537search11
  Interpretation:  channel addition is not intrinsically complementary or cannibalizing; the mechanism depends on cross-channel interaction and source of sales.
  Exception 3 — Service measurement
  Field and laboratory experiments show that post-service experience surveys can themselves alter subsequent purchase timing. citeturn999537search14
  Interpretation:  measurement is not always a neutral observation layer. In some designs, the measurement instrument can become an intervention/antecedent.
  Exception 4 — Permission / privacy
  Privacy concerns can reduce permission granting, while content relevance or entertainment can mitigate the effect. citeturn999537search6
  Interpretation:  digital permission mechanisms require benefit–risk trade-off fields rather than a single privacy-risk variable.
  Exception Audit Result
  Passed as boundary detection.  No exception requires deleting or replacing the core mechanism architecture. Instead, the exceptions demonstrate why the architecture requires explicit moderators, mediators, measurement conditions, and context. Apparent contradictions were reclassified as context-dependent mechanisms where the evidence supports that interpretation.

Cycle A — Pricing / Value
Zeithaml (1988) links price, perceived quality and perceived value within a means-end framework; the usable mechanism is perception/evaluation rather than a simple price→purchase rule. citeturn527939search5
Boundary:  value perception depends on the consumer/context; do not equate price with value or infer universal willingness-to-pay effects from this model.
Cycle B — Channels / Distribution
Stern & Reve (1980) model distribution channels as political economies that integrate economic and sociopolitical determinants of channel-member behavior. citeturn510320search0
Boundary:  channel outcomes require system-level variables such as governance, power, conflict/cooperation and environment; firm-level campaign metrics are insufficient to represent the whole channel system.
Cycle C — Innovation / Adoption
Peres, Muller & Mahajan (2010) review diffusion models that incorporate interpersonal communication, network effects and social signals, indicating that adoption can be shaped by interaction/network mechanisms rather than exposure alone. citeturn527939search0
Boundary:  diffusion mechanisms vary by market, technology generation and network context.
Cycle D — CRM / Relationship Practice
Hillebrand, Nijholt & Nijssen (2011) use institutional theory to explain variation in CRM effectiveness; their empirical study of 107 organizations reports lower customer insights when CRM adoption is driven by mimetic institutional motives, while strategic alignment can buffer the effect. citeturn572151search0turn572151search3
Boundary:  this is evidence about adoption motives, institutional pressure and CRM effectiveness in the studied context, not a universal claim about all CRM programs.
Cycle E — Services / Value Logic
Vargo & Lusch (2004) frame marketing around service provision, intangible resources, relationships and value co-creation rather than a goods/transaction-only logic. citeturn572151search1
Boundary:  this is a theoretical lens; it does not replace transaction, consumer-decision or channel theories where their focal mechanisms are more appropriate.

Cross-Layer Mechanism / Dependency Matrix
Derived operating rules
- Mechanism first:  identify the causal/explanatory mechanism before selecting a tactic or metric.
- Level matching:  a construct must be measured at the same level as the claim it supports.
- Dependency declaration:  every mechanism requires its key contextual dependencies to be recorded.
- Technology as overlay:  MarTech/AI changes execution possibilities but does not erase underlying behavioral, organizational, channel or institutional mechanisms.
- No automatic promotion:  repeated conceptual support does not by itself justify a universal Principle or causal claim.
